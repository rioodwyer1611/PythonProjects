1. Let the cyclic group {[0], [1], [2], ..., [n − 1]} be denoted by Z/nZ. Consider the following statement: for every positive integer n and every x in Z/nZ, there exists y ∈ Z/nZ such that xy = [1].
(a) (2 marks) Write the negation of this statement.

Solution: The negation is: There exists a positive integer n and x ∈ Z/nZ such that for all
y ∈ Z/nZ, xy 6= 1.
No part mark for this question.

(b) (2 marks) Is the original statement true or false? Justify your answer.

Solution: The negation is the correct statement. For instance, take n = 4 and x = [2]. Then x
has no multiplicative inverse in Z/4Z.
No part mark. If they get it right, they get 2. If not they get 0.

2. (4 marks) Let x be a fixed positive integer. Is it possible to have a graph G with 4x + 1 vertices
such that G has a vertex of degree d for all d = 1, 2, ..., 4x + 1? Justify your answer.
(Note: The graph G does not need to be simple.)

Solution: No. If such a graph existed, then the vertices of degree 1, 3, ..., 4n + 1 are all odd.
There are 2n + 1 such vertices. Thus, such a graph would have odd number of odd vertices,
which is a contradiction to the Handshake Theorem.
2 marks for answering No.
2 marks for providing correct reasoning.

(b) (2 marks) Determine the equivalence classes.
