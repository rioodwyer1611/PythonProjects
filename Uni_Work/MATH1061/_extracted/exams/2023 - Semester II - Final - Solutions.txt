(a) (2 marks) Write down the quotient and remainder when −27 is divided by 6, according to
the quotient-remainder theorem.

Solution: −27 = 6 × −5 + 3. Thus, the remainder is 3 and the quotient is −5.

(b) (2 marks) Determine whether the following statement is true or false. Justify your reasoning.
For all positive integers a and b, we have
gcd(a + b, a − b) = gcd(a, b).

Solution: The statement is false. For instance, gcd(3, 1) = 1 6= 2 = gcd(3 + 1, 3 − 1).

(c) (2 marks) Compute gcd(112, 30). Show your work.

Solution: Note that 112 = 24 × 7 and 30 = 2 × 3 × 5. Thus, the only common factor is 2;
i.e. gcd(112, 30) = 2.

4. (6 marks) Let f : N → R be a function. Prove that the image of N under f is countable.

Solution: For each y in the image of f , choose exactly one element in N which maps to y. Let
A be the subset of N consisting of these chosen elements. Note that A is countable, because it
is a subset of N. Now the restriction of f to A defines a bijection between A and the image of
f . Thus, the image of f is also countable.
Alternate solution: Let g : N → f (N) be defined by g(n) := f (n). i.e. g is the same as f
but the codomain is restricted. By definition, g is surjective. Therefore |N| ≥ |f (N)|.

For example, f (7) = 4 because 72 = 49 is congruent to 4 modulo 5.

(b) (2 marks) What is the preimage of 1? Justify your answer

Solution: The preimage is equal to the set integers of the form 5k ± 1. Indeed, every such
integer is in the preimage because f (5k ± 1) = 1, and no other integer is in the preimage
because f (5k) = 0 and f (5k ± 2) = 4.

Solution: f is not one to one because, for instance, f (1) = f (−1), but 1 6= −1.

Solution: From (b), we know that f has image {0, 1, 4}. Therefore f is not onto because 3
is in the codomain of f but not the image.

9. (7 marks) A group of 10 friends are attending the movies together and insist on sitting in one
row. There are exactly ten seats in each row.

(a) (2 marks) Two of the friends (Bailey and Courtney) insist on sitting next to each other.
In how many different ways can the 10 be seated while satisfying this wish? Justify your
answer.

Solution: We treat the two people sitting side-by-side as one person. This gives 9! ways
for the 10 people to sit. But for each of these ways, the two people who insisted on sitting
side-by-side can be swapped, still remaining side-by-side and yielding a new way to sit.
Therefore there are 2 · 9! ways.

(b) (2 marks) Now suppose Bailey and Courtney no longer insist on sitting next to each other.
However, there are two other people, Tara and Adam, who do not like each other and do
not want to sit next to each other. In how many different ways can the 10 be seated while
satisfying this wish? Justify your answer.

Solution: There are 10! ways for the group to sit. Out of these 10! ways, 2 · 9! ways will have
them sitting side-by-side. Therefore there are 10! − 2 · 9! ways where they are not sitting
side-by-side.

(c) (3 marks) Finally, suppose Bailey and Courtney want to sit next to each other and, at the
same time, Tara and Adam do not want to sit next to each other. In how many different
ways can the 10 be seated while satisfying these wishes? Justify your answer.

Solution: We treat Bailey and Courtney as one person. So then we have to sit 9 people 2 of
whom don’t want to sit next to each other. By the previous part, the total is 9! − 2 · 8!. But
we can also permute the location of Bailey and Courtney; thus, the total is 2(9! − 2 · 8!).
