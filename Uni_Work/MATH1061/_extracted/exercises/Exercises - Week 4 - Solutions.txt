Problems. Prove or disprove the following statements:
(1) ∀ m ∈ Z, ∃ n ∈ Z, such that m + n is odd and mn is even.

(3 marks)

The statement is true and we use a direct proof to show this.
Proof. If m is even, then m = 2k for some k ∈ Z. Choose n = m + 1 = 2k + 1
which is an integer since k ∈ Z.
Then m + n = 2k + 2k + 1 = 2(2k) + 1 and mn = 2k(2k + 1) = 2(2k 2 + k).
Since k ∈ Z, we know that 2k ∈ Z and (2k + 1) ∈ Z. Therefore, m + n is odd and
mn is even.
If m is odd, then m = 2k + 1 for some k ∈ Z. Choose n = m − 1 = 2k. Then
m + n = 2(2k) + 1 and mn = 2(2k + 1) as before, and so they are odd and even
respectively.
□
MARKING:
1 mark for mentioning that the statement is true.
2 marks for the proof of the statement (1 mark if the proof is in the right direction,
but some mistakes are made). No marks if only an example for a specific m is
given.

(3) There is no smallest positive real number.

(4 marks)

This statement is true. It can be written as
∀x ∈ R>0 , ∃y ∈ R>0 such that y < x
or equivalently as
∼ (∃x ∈ R>0 such that ∀y ∈ R>0 , x ≤ y) .
To prove this statement, take any x ∈ R>0 . Let y = x2 . Then y is a positive
real number and y < x.
