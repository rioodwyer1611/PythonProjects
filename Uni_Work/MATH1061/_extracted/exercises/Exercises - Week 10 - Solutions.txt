(1) Define a relation ρ on Z≥1 by aρb if
gcd(a, 144) = gcd(b, 144).
(a) (3 marks) Prove that ρ is an equivalence relation.
Reflexive: For any a ∈ Z≥1 , gcd(a, 144) = gcd(a, 144), and so aρa.
Symmetric: For any a, b ∈ Z≥1 , suppose that aρb. This means that gcd(a, 144) =
gcd(b, 144), so gcd(b, 144) = gcd(a, 144), which implies that bρa.
Transitive: For any a, b, c ∈ Z≥1 , suppose that aρb and bρc. This means that
gcd(a, 144) = gcd(b, 144), so gcd(b, 144) = gcd(c, 144). Together, these two
imply that gcd(a, 144) = gcd(c, 144), and so aρc.
MARKING:
1 mark for a valid proof of reflexivity
1 mark for a valid proof of symmetry
1 mark for a valid proof of transitivity
(b) (2 marks) Determine the equivalence classes. How many equivalence classes
are there? Justify your answer.
For each positive divisor d of 144, there is an equivalence class of the form
[d] = {a ∈ Z≥1 | gcd(a, 144) = d}.

As 144 = 24 × 32 , there are (4 + 1) × (2 + 1) = 15 distinct positive divisors
of 144. This means that there can be at most 15 equivalence classes.
Furthermore, for each positive divisor a of 144, gcd(a, 144) = a. So if a and
b are distinct positive divisors of 144, a̸ ρ b, and so they are in different equivalence classes. Therefore there are at least 15 equivalence classes (and hence
exactly 15).
MARKING:
1 mark for correctly describing the equivalence classes.
1 mark for identifying that there are 15 equivalence classes with some justification
Note for this question, stating that there is an equivalence class for each
positive divisor and that there are (4 + 1) × (2 + 1) = 15 positive divisors of
144 is sufficient justification.

string is the number of ones it contains, for example weight(110110) = 4. Define
a relation ρ on S by
sρt

Is ρ a partial order? Justify your answer.
The relation is not antisymmetric. Let s = 100000 and t = 000001. Then
weight(s) = 1 = weight(t). Thus, sρt and tρs but s ̸= t.
Since it is not antisymmetric, the relation is not a partial order.

(3) (2 marks) Determine whether Z10 − {0} is a group under multiplication.
This is not a group. Consider the elements 2 and 5 in Z10 − {0}. Under the
operation of multiplication,
2 × 5 ≡ 10 ≡ 0

(mod 10),

which is not in Z10 − {0}. Thus this is not closed, and therefore not a group.
