Basic Concepts
Task 1.

Strings and Languages

Let Σ = {a, b} and let
L = {w ∈ Σ∗ | |w| ≥ 1, and w starts and ends with the same symbol}.
(a) List five strings in L and five strings not in L.
(b) Describe L (the complement of L) in plain English.

Solution.
(a) In L: a, b, aa, bb, aba, bab, abba, baab. (Any non-empty string whose first and last
characters match.)
Not in L: ε, ab, ba, aab, bba, abbb.
(b) L = Σ∗ \ L consists of two kinds of strings:
• The empty string ε (which is not in L since |w| ≥ 1 is required), and
• all non-empty strings over {a, b} whose first and last characters are different (i.e.,
strings that begin with a and end with b, or begin with b and end with a).
Worth asking students whether ε ∈ L before they describe the complement. They should
be comfortable with the idea of seeing ε as a string (i.e., ε ∈ Σ∗ ).

Task 2.

Strings and Languages

Let Σ = {a, b}, L1 = {w ∈ Σ∗ | w starts with a}, and L2 = {w ∈ Σ∗ | w ends with b}.

1

(a) List five strings in L1 ∩ L2 .
(b) List five strings in L1 ∪ L2 .

Solution.
(a) L1 ∩ L2 contains strings that start with a and end with b.
Examples: ab, aab, abb, abab, aaabb.
(b) L1 ∪ L2 contains strings that start with a or end with b (or both).
Examples: a, ab, bb, aab, bbb.
Make sure students are comfortable with the idea of seeing languages as sets of strings.
