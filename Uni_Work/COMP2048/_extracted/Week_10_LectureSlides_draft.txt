Classical vs. quantum computability
The Turing machine is a sufficiently general model for computation to capture entirely that which can
reasonably be thought of as mathematically computable.
Definition (Church-Turing Thesis CTT)
Any function that can be computed by an algorithm can be computed by a Turing Machine.
Definition (A quantum computer)
A quantum computer is a device that uses the principles of quantum mechanics to perform computation.
Are quantum computers Turing machines?
• Pauli Benioff (1980) and David Deutsch (1985) argued that any quantum computing device is still
describable as a Turing machine. They established a correspondence between the time evolution of a
quantum system and a Universal Turing Machine.
• These papers established an equivalence of computability between classical Turing machines and
quantum computing, upholding the Church-Turing thesis

Quantum postulates
1.

All knowledge of a system is contained in an abstract quantum state, Ψ .

2.

We cannot directly observe this Ψ , or how Ψ evolves in time i.e. the time evolution of a system is
distinct from observing a system in quantum physics

3.

Observations are inherently random and perfect measurements collapse Ψ to take a known value

4.

Uncertainty ‘protects’ quantum mechanical behavior of a system.

Quantum mechanics give us a manner to allocate probabilities to observations using information in Ψ .
